# California HOA & Condo Insurance in 2026

California has one of the hardest association-insurance markets in the country. Years of severe wildfire losses pushed major carriers to pull back from new business, drove more communities onto the state FAIR Plan, and sent HOA and condo master-policy premiums sharply higher. Here is what changed, where the market stands in 2026, and what every California board should do before its next renewal.

## Why California association premiums jumped

Wildfire catastrophe losses reshaped the entire California property market. Since 2023 several of the largest carriers restricted or paused new homeowners and commercial-property business in wildfire-exposed areas, and the reinsurance that backs association master policies grew far more expensive. Communities in the wildland-urban interface — the foothills and canyon edges around most metros — felt it first, but the pullback rippled into pricing statewide. Fewer carriers competing for your program means higher premiums and, just as important, higher wind and fire deductibles.

## The FAIR Plan and the DIC "wrap"

As admitted carriers retreated, more associations landed on the California FAIR Plan, the state's insurer of last resort. The FAIR Plan is a backstop, not a deal: it provides basic fire coverage with real limits and gaps, so boards typically pair it with a separate [difference-in-conditions (DIC) policy](/coverage-gap-review.html) to rebuild full protection — liability, water, theft, and more. That combination is more expensive and has more moving parts, and it is easy to end up with a gap between the two policies. If your association is on a FAIR Plan plus wrap, confirm the two actually fit together.

## California's regulatory reset

Under the state's Sustainable Insurance Strategy, regulators now let carriers use forward-looking catastrophe models and reinsurance costs in their pricing in exchange for commitments to write more coverage in wildfire-distressed areas. The intent is to bring admitted carriers back over time. For a board, the practical takeaway is that the market is shifting month to month — a program that had only one option a year ago may have new options now, which is exactly why re-shopping before you renew matters more in California than almost anywhere else.

## The replacement-cost trap

Rebuild costs climbed sharply, but many California master policies still carry valuations set before that run-up — which leaves the association underinsured. If a total loss triggers a coinsurance penalty, the shortfall falls on the association and its owners. A current [replacement-cost analysis](/hoa-replacement-cost-analysis.html) is one of the highest-value things a California board can do before renewal, and it also strengthens your position with carriers.

## What California boards should do before renewal

Start at least 90 days out. Get a current replacement-cost appraisal, and document your wildfire mitigation — defensible space and home-hardening increasingly affect both eligibility and price. Then benchmark the full program — the [master policy](/hoa-master-insurance-policies.html), the DIC wrap, and [directors & officers (D&O) coverage](/hoa-board-liability-insurance.html) — across carriers, not just the premium number. In a market moving this fast, the board that shops has leverage the board that auto-renews does not.
