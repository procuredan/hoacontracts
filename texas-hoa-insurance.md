# Texas HOA & Condo Insurance in 2026

Texas associations face some of the steepest property premiums in the country — hail across the north, windstorm on the coast, and one of the largest HOA footprints of any state. The pressure is real, but the detail that catches the most boards off guard is not the premium; it is the deductible. Here is what is driving Texas association pricing in 2026 and what your board should check before renewal.

## Hail and wind are driving Texas premiums

North and Central Texas sit in one of the most hail-battered corridors in the nation, and repeated severe storms have driven heavy carrier losses and steady rate increases for associations. On the Gulf Coast, named-storm and windstorm exposure adds a second layer. The result is a market where master-policy premiums have risen fast — and where carriers are managing their risk as much through deductible structure as through price.

## Watch your wind/hail deductible — it may be a percentage

This is the one every Texas board should check: many master policies carry a percentage wind/hail deductible — often 1% to 5% of the insured value — rather than a flat dollar amount. On a large community that can be a six-figure number the association has to absorb before coverage responds after a storm. Boards routinely discover this only when a claim lands. Read your [deductible structure](/hoa-insurance-deductibles.html) now, and make sure a [coverage-gap review](/coverage-gap-review.html) models it in real dollars against your buildings.

## Coastal associations and TWIA

In the designated coastal counties, standard wind coverage can be hard to place, and the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA) serves as the fallback for wind and hail — with its own limits and requirements. Coastal boards often pair TWIA with additional layers to rebuild full protection, and the fit between those policies is exactly where gaps hide.

## Replacement cost in a high-inflation rebuild market

Texas construction costs rose sharply, and master policies with stale valuations are commonly underinsured. A coinsurance penalty at a total loss falls on the association and its owners, so a current [replacement-cost analysis](/hoa-replacement-cost-analysis.html) is worth doing before you approach the market — it protects the community and strengthens your submission.

## What Texas boards should do before renewal

Model your wind/hail deductible in dollars, not percentages. Get an updated appraisal. Then benchmark the [master policy](/hoa-master-insurance-policies.html) and [board D&O](/hoa-board-liability-insurance.html) across carriers, and start early — coastal and hail-exposed programs take longer to place. The goal is simple: no surprises in the deductible, and no premium you did not test against the market.
